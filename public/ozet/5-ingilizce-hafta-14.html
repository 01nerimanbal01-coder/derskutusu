    Bu hafta

    In Week 14 of «Theme 3: Personal Life», we bring together all three sub-themes of our unit: «Basic body parts and physical features; clothes; daily routines and activities». We learn how to name parts of the human body, describe what people look like using «to be» and «have got / has got», compare physical features and clothes using comparative adjectives, and talk about what we wear and what we do every day using the Simple Present Tense, frequency adverbs, and question tags.

    Basic Body Parts and Physical Features Vocabulary

    To describe a person accurately in English, we first need to know the target nouns for basic body parts: a head (baş), a face (yüz), hair (saç), an eye (göz), a nose (burun), a neck (boyun), an arm (kol), a hand (el), a finger (el parmağı), a leg (bacak), a foot (ayak), a tooth (diş), a body (vücut) and height (boy uzunluğu). Most body parts form their plural by adding «-s» (two eyes, two arms, ten fingers, two legs), whereas tooth becomes teeth (dişler) and foot becomes feet (ayaklar). In addition, hair is used as an uncountable singular noun in English (e.g., «Her hair is long»).

    We combine these nouns with descriptive adjectives for physical features. For height and body build, we use tall (uzun boylu), short (kısa boylu), thin (zayıf/ince) and average (orta boylu/yapılı). For hair style and length, we use straight (düz), wavy (dalgalı), curly (kıvırcık), long (uzun) and short (kısa). For face shapes, we use round (yuvarlak), oval (oval) and square (kare).

    Irregular Plurals and Hair Rule

    One tooth - thirty-two teeth (diş - dişler); one foot - two feet (ayak - ayaklar). «Hair» (saç) tekil kabul edilir, «a hair» veya «hairs» denmez: «She has got wavy hair.»

    Hair and Face Adjectives

    Saç şekilleri: straight (düz), wavy (dalgalı), curly (kıvırcık). Yüz şekilleri: round (yuvarlak), oval (oval), square (kare).

    Describing Physical Appearance: To Be vs. Have Got and Has Got

    When we ask about someone's physical appearance, we use the question «What does he / she look like?» (O nasıl görünür? / Fiziksel görünüşü nasıldır?). Do not confuse this with «What is he/she like?» which asks about personality. When answering, we use two different verb structures depending on whether we use an adjective alone or a noun phrase.

    We use am / is / are before height and build adjectives: «I am tall and thin», «He is of average height», «They are short». On the other hand, we use have got (with I, you, we, they) and has got (with he, she, it) when describing specific body parts such as eyes, hair, face, or nose: «She has got curly brown hair and green eyes», «He has got a round face and a small nose».

    Is vs. Has Got Ayrımı

    Boy ve kilo sıfatlarıyla «am/is/are» kullanılır (He is tall and thin); saç, göz ve yüz gibi isim tamlamalarıyla «have got / has got» kullanılır (He has got blue eyes).

    Appearance Question Pattern

    «What does Kerem look like?» — «He is tall and thin. He has got short straight hair and brown eyes.»

    Clothes, Accessories and Seasonal Clothing Habits

    Our personal life also includes the clothes and accessories we wear in different seasons and occasions. For everyday clothing, our target vocabulary includes a T-shirt (tişört), a shirt (gömlek), a jumper / a sweater (kazak), a jacket (ceket), a coat (kaban/mont), a raincoat (yağmurluk), a dress (elbise), a skirt (etek), trousers / pants (pantolon), jeans (kot pantolon), shorts (şort), socks (çoraplar), shoes (ayakkabılar), boots (bot/çizme) and a (fancy dress) costume (kostüm).

    We complete our outfits with accessories and jewellery: a belt (kemer), a hat (şapka), a cap (kasketli şapka), a scarf (atkı/fular), a headscarf (başörtüsü), gloves (eldiven), glasses (gözlük), sunglasses (güneş gözlüğü), a bag / a handbag (çanta / el çantası), an umbrella (şemsiye), a watch (kol saati), a ring (yüzük), a necklace (kolye), earrings (küpe) and silver (gümüş) items. Notice that words with two parts like trousers, jeans, shorts, glasses, sunglasses, shoes, boots, gloves, socks, earrings are plural and take «are» (e.g., «My jeans are blue»).

    British and American English Pairs

    Trousers (BrE) = Pants (AmE) [Pantolon]; Jumper (BrE) = Sweater (AmE) [Kazak]. Her iki kullanım da müfredatta yer alır.

    Plural Clothing Nouns (Çift Parçalı Giysiler)

    Trousers, jeans, shorts, glasses, sunglasses, shoes, boots, socks, gloves ve earrings çoğul kullanılır; tek bir tanesini belirtmek için «a pair of jeans / shoes» denir.

    Possessive Adjectives and Comparative Adjectives

    To state who owns an item of clothing or whose body part we are describing, we use possessive adjectives before nouns: my (benim), your (senin/sizin), his (onun - erkek), her (onun - kız), its (onun - cansız/hayvan), our (bizim), and their (onların). For example: «His jacket is black, and her handbag is brown.»

    When we compare the physical features or clothes of two people, we use comparative adjectives followed by than. One-syllable adjectives take «-er»: tall - taller than, short - shorter than, long - longer than. Adjectives ending in consonant-vowel-consonant double the last letter: thin - thinner than, big - bigger than. Adjectives ending in «-y» change to «-ier»: curly - curlier than. Long adjectives with two or more syllables take «more»: more comfortable than, more colourful than. Irregular adjectives change completely: good - better than, bad - worse than.

    Adjective TypeBase FormComparative FormExample Comparison Sentence
    Short (1 syllable)tall / short / youngtaller / shorter / younger thanBurak is taller than his brother.
    CVC Doublingthin / big / hotthinner / bigger / hotter thanA T-shirt is thinner than a winter coat.
    Ending in -ycurly / wavy / earlycurlier / wavier / earlier thanSelin's hair is curlier than my hair.
    Long (2+ syllables)comfortable / fashionablemore comfortable / fashionable thanTrainers are more comfortable than boots.

    Comparative Spelling Rules

    tall -> taller than · thin -> thinner than (çift n) · curly -> curlier than (-ier) · comfortable -> more comfortable than · good -> better than.

    His vs. Her Usage

    Erkek bir kişinin eşyası veya özelliği için «his» (Ali's cap = his cap), kız bir kişinin eşyası için «her» (Ece's necklace = her necklace) kullanılır.

    Daily Routines, Activities, Time Expressions and Frequency Adverbs

    To talk about our daily routines and free-time activities, we use the Simple Present Tense. With I, you, we, they, we use the base form of the verb (wake up, wash, brush, get dressed, have, go, do, sleep). With third-person singular subjects (he, she, it), we add -s, -es, or -ies to the verb in affirmative sentences (wakes up, washes, brushes, gets dressed, has breakfast, goes to school, does homework, studies). In negative and question sentences, we use don't / doesn't and Do / Does with the base verb.

    We express how often we do an activity with frequency adverbs: always (%100 - her zaman), usually (%80 - genellikle), often (%60 - sık sık), sometimes (%40 - bazen), and never (%0 - asla). They go before main verbs («She always brushes her teeth») but after am/is/are («He is never late»). For time expressions, we use at with clock times and night (at 7 o'clock, at half past seven, at quarter to eight, at night), in with parts of the day and seasons (in the morning, in winter), and on with days (on Mondays, on Sunday morning).

    Time Question / PrepositionRule & UsageExample Sentence
    What time ...? / ATClock times, noon, night, the weekendShe wakes up at quarter past seven (07:15).
    When ...? / INParts of the day, months, seasonsI do my homework in the afternoon.
    When ...? / ONDays of the week, specific datesWe have sports club on Wednesdays.
    How often ...?Frequency adverbs (always, usually, often...)I usually wear a jumper in autumn.
    Third-Person Singular (-s / -es / has)

    He/She/It öznelerinde: have -> has (She has breakfast); go -> goes; wash -> washes; brush -> brushes; watch -> watches; study -> studies.

    Never in Affirmative Structure

    «Never» cümleye olumsuz anlam katar ancak olumlu cümle yapısıyla kullanılır; «doesn't never» denmez: «He never wears a hat.»

    Question Tags and Pronunciation of Final -s / -es Sounds

    In everyday conversation, we use question tags («değil mi?») at the end of a statement to confirm information or ask for agreement. The golden rule is: a positive statement takes a negative tag, and a negative statement takes a positive tag. With «to be», we repeat the form of be: «She is tall, isn't she?», «Your boots aren't new, are they?». With Simple Present verbs, we use don't / doesn't or do / does: «You wear glasses, don't you?», «He gets up early, doesn't he?», «She doesn't like rings, does she?».

    In pronunciation (ENG.5.3.P1), the final -s / -es in plural nouns (like clothes and body parts) and third-person singular verbs is pronounced in three different ways: 1) /s/ after voiceless sounds (p, t, k, f): caps, coats, boots, shirts, skirts, socks, walks, gets. 2) /z/ after vowels and voiced sounds (b, d, g, l, m, n, r, v): shoes, jeans, gloves, bags, rings, eyes, legs, wears, goes. 3) /ız/ after sibilant sounds (s, sh, ch, x, z): dresses, glasses, sunglasses, watches, necklaces, brushes, washes.

    Ending SoundTrigger Final SoundsClothing & Body ExamplesRoutine Verb Examples
    /s/-p, -t, -k, -f (voiceless)caps, coats, boots, shirts, skirts, socksgets, wakes, puts on, walks
    /z/vowels & -b, -d, -g, -l, -m, -n, -r, -vshoes, jeans, gloves, bags, rings, eyes, armswears, goes, has, reads, plays
    /ız/-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z, -ce, -gedresses, glasses, watches, necklacesbrushes, washes, watches, dresses
    Question Tag Golden Rule

    Olumlu cümle + Olumsuz tag (+ , -?): «He has got brown eyes, hasn't he?» / «She wears a scarf, doesn't she?». Olumsuz cümle + Olumlu tag (- , +?).

    /s/, /z/ and /ız/ Ending Clue

    Kelime -ss, -sh, -ch, -ce, -ge ile bitiyorsa -es eki ekstra bir hece olarak /ız/ okunur: dresses, glasses, watches, brushes, washes.

    Örnekler

    1. Örnek 1«What does your best friend Elif look like?» sorusunu Elif'in uzun boylu, zayıf, uzun dalgalı kahverengi saçlı ve yeşil gözlü olduğunu belirterek İngilizce cevaplayınız.

      Cevabı göster

      «She is tall and thin. She has got long wavy brown hair and green eyes.» (Boy ve yapı için «is», saç ve göz için «has got» kullanılır.)

    2. Örnek 2«Soğuk ve yağmurlu bir kış gününde dışarı çıkarken ne giyersin ve yanına ne alırsın?» sorusuna hedef giysi ve aksesuar kelimelerinden en az dördünü kullanarak İngilizce bir cümle kurunuz.

      Cevabı göster

      «On a cold and rainy day, I wear a raincoat, a warm jumper, boots, and gloves, and I take my umbrella.»

    3. Örnek 3Verilen bilgileri karşılaştırma sıfatlarıyla (comparative adjectives) birleştiriniz: 1) Mert (158 cm) / Can (150 cm) [tall] — 2) Boots / Trainers [comfortable].

      Cevabı göster

      1) «Mert is taller than Can.» (Kısa sıfata -er eklenir.) — 2) «Trainers are more comfortable than boots.» (Uzun sıfata more eklenir.)

    4. Örnek 4Aşağıdaki cümlelerde boş bırakılan yerlere uygun zaman edatlarını (at / in / on) ve fiil çekimlerini yerleştiriniz: «Beren usually ___ (wake up) ___ half past seven ___ the morning ___ weekdays.»

      Cevabı göster

      «Beren usually wakes up at half past seven in the morning on weekdays.» (Beren tekil özne olduğu için wakes up; saatlerde at, günün bölümlerinde in, günlerde on kullanılır.)

    5. Örnek 5Aşağıdaki cümlelerin sonuna uygun «question tag» (değil mi?) ifadelerini ekleyiniz: 1) «She wears glasses, ...?» — 2) «Your shoes aren't black, ...?»

      Cevabı göster

      1) «She wears glasses, doesn't she?» (Geniş zaman olumlu tekil cümle -> olumsuz tag: doesn't she?) — 2) «Your shoes aren't black, are they?» (Çoğul olumsuz to be cümlesi -> olumlu tag: are they?)

    6. Örnek 6«coats, watches, gloves, shirts, dresses, shoes» kelimelerini sonlarındaki «-s / -es» ekinin okunuşuna (/s/, /z/, /ız/) göre üç gruba ayırınız.

      Cevabı göster

      1) /s/ sesiyle okunanlar: coats, shirts. — 2) /z/ sesiyle okunanlar: gloves, shoes. — 3) /ız/ sesiyle okunanlar: watches, dresses.

    Kısaca

    • Basic body parts include head, face, eye, nose, neck, arm, hand, finger, leg, foot (plural: feet), tooth (plural: teeth), hair, body, and height.
    • Physical appearance is described with «am/is/are» for height and build (tall, short, thin, average) and «have got / has got» for hair (curly, wavy, straight), eyes, and face shape (round, oval, square).
    • Clothing and accessories vocabulary covers seasonal outfits (coat, raincoat, jumper/sweater, jacket, T-shirt, shirt, dress, skirt, trousers/pants, jeans, shorts, boots, shoes, scarf, gloves, umbrella, sunglasses, watch, ring, necklace, earrings).
    • Comparative adjectives (-er than / more ... than) compare two people or items, while possessive adjectives (my, your, his, her, its, our, their) show ownership before nouns.
    • Daily routines use the Simple Present Tense with frequency adverbs (always, usually, often, sometimes, never), time prepositions (at/in/on), question tags, and accurate /s/, /z/, /ız/ pronunciation.
